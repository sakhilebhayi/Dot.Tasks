# R-36 Dot.Tasks: design study and direction (2026-11-05)

The owner delegated the design choice. This records what was considered and why. The product notes come from general knowledge of these products, not from hands-on testing of each one here.

## What the leading products teach

| Product | What it gets right | Where it costs people |
|---|---|---|
| Linear | Speed, the keyboard, one way to do each thing | Assumes a software team; little for an operational business |
| Things | Today is a calm, ordered list. Finishing feels good | Personal only, nothing for a team |
| Todoist | Natural-language quick add that reads dates | Lists alone, thin for managers |
| Asana | Many views over one record | Heavy, with a lot of fields and rows of chrome |
| Trello | The board is obvious, and dragging is the product | Falls apart past the board: no real list or plan |
| ClickUp | Does everything | Does everything, so every screen is crowded |
| Monday | Colour makes status scannable | The colour is so loud it stops meaning anything |
| Jira | Workflows and control | The administration the brief says to remove |
| Notion | Flexibility | You have to build the tool before using it |
| Microsoft Planner | Simple, and it's already there | Shallow |

## What Dot.Tasks takes from that

- From Things and Linear: a calm Today, and speed everywhere.
- From Todoist: typing a sentence is the fastest way to make a task. Decision 2 means this works with no AI.
- From Trello: direct manipulation is the product, not an extra.
- From Asana: one record, many views.
- It leaves behind: mandatory fields, loud colour, and configuring before you can work.

## The opening none of them takes

They all design for software teams or knowledge workers. Dot.Tasks serves mines, workshops and offices, and those places already have a tool people trust: the T-card rack, where a job card slides along a board by hand. That gives the product an idea no competitor can share, and it fits the brief's demand that the platform feel responsive to the user's hands.

## Directions considered

| Direction | Idea | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **The Job Card** | The T-card rack. Cards with weight, on stone, with a week rack | **Chosen.** It comes from the customer's own world and makes drag and drop the concept, not a feature |
| The Day Sheet | A printed shift sheet, ruled, read top to bottom | Strong for My Day, weak for boards, and close to Dot.Sheets' language |
| The Checklist | Tick marks as the whole language | Clean, but any task app could say it |

Dot.Forms has the carbon book, Dot.HR the register, Dot.Projects the programme and Dot.Sheets the dot grid. The job card sits beside them as this product's own object.
